abstract type Plasma.AbstractPlasmaScheme ... defines an abstract type to distinguish different kinds of plasma computations; see also:

+ struct AverageAtomScheme
    ... to perform an average-atom computation.
+ struct LineShiftScheme
    ... to compute the energy shifts and properties of atomic/ionic lines in some selected plasma model.
+ struct SahaBoltzmannScheme
    ... to compute thermodynamic properties of a Saha-Boltzmann LTE mixture.
+ struct SatelliteDiagnosticScheme
    ... to compute a dielectronic-satellite-to-parent-line intensity-ratio Te diagnostic.
+ struct CollisionalRadiativeScheme
    ... to compute the (relative) collisional-radiative population balance among a small set of
        levels of one ion.
+ struct KineticChargeBalanceScheme
    ... the cross-charge-state sibling of CollisionalRadiativeScheme: a kinetic (rate-based, non-LTE)
        population balance across several adjacent charge states, with PhotoEmission + ImpactExcitation
        within each stage and ImpactIonization + PhotoRecombination + ThreeBodyRecombination between
        adjacent stages. Each of the five legs independently selects an Empirical (tabulated/fitted) or
        a structure-based (ab-initio/semi-empirical) treatment -- see Plasma.AbstractRateTreatment.
+ struct SuperConfigurationOpacityScheme
    ... to build one SuperConfigurations.Group per reference configuration, report its Boltzmann
        population at a given plasma temperature, build a SuperConfigurations.TransitionArray for every
        E1-connected pair with its oscillator-strength sum, and -- if opacityOmegas is given -- sum
        every array's Gaussian onto that frequency grid into a photo-absorption cross section
        kappa(omega). All three stages are built; see that struct's own docstring for what even that
        still is not (a macroscopic opacity in cm^2/g).
+ struct SuperConfigurationIonizationScheme
    ... to compute the EQUILIBRIUM (LTE) population across several ionization stages of one species,
        each stage described by its own SuperConfigurations.Group's rather than by diagonalized levels
        -- the natural extension of SuperConfigurationOpacityScheme ACROSS charge states instead of
        within one. See that struct's own docstring for the grand-canonical Saha-Boltzmann equations
        and their self-consistent electron-density solve.
+ struct SuperConfigurationMixtureOpacityScheme
    ... to combine several SPECIES (each its own Nuclear.Model, own number fraction, and now possibly
        several of its own ionization STAGES) into one coupled multi-species Saha-Boltzmann balance and
        one PLANCK MEAN opacity at a shared LTE temperature -- the natural extension of
        SuperConfigurationOpacityScheme ACROSS a composition AND of SuperConfigurationIonizationScheme
        ACROSS species, both axes at once. OPTIONALLY (`boundFreeApprox`, 18-Oct-2026) also sums a
        bound-free (photoionization) contribution between every pair of adjacent stages, reusing
        `Empirical.photoionizationCrossSection`, and OPTIONALLY (`freeFreeIncluded`, 19-Oct-2026) also
        sums a free-free (inverse-bremsstrahlung) contribution per stage, via
        `Empirical.freeFreeAbsorptionCoefficient` -- once EITHER is on, the ROSSELAND mean, previously
        withheld as ill-posed, becomes well-defined and is reported too. See that struct's own
        docstring for the coupled balance, the two-stage mixing rule, both continuum additions, and the
        Planck/Rosseland weight functions.

JenaAtomicCalculator.Plasma.AbstractRateTreatment — Type

abstract type Plasma.AbstractRateTreatment ... distinguishes, for ONE leg (one process) of a Plasma.KineticChargeBalanceScheme, the QUALITY of both the atomic input (scaled/hydrogenic, or a real JAC-computed energy/rate) and the collision/radiative dynamics model (a literature-fitted formula, or JAC's own cross-section computation) used for that rate. Every leg (PhotoEmission, ImpactExcitation, ImpactIonization, PhotoRecombination, ThreeBodyRecombination) carries its own AbstractRateTreatment, so a single scheme can mix the three tiers freely across legs ("mixed use") – this is the one thing the whole scheme is built to make visible. The type is deliberately kept separate from Empirical.AbstractEmpiricalApproximation (it is NOT a subtype of it): the three tiers are different in kind, not flavours of the same thing.

+ Plasma.EmpiricalTreatment        ... scaled/hydrogenic input AND a fitted formula; wraps one
                                         Empirical.AbstractEmpiricalApproximation singleton; no SCF/CI anywhere.
+ Plasma.SemiEmpiricalTreatment    ... a REAL, JAC-computed input (a cheap mean-field energy, Einstein-A or
                                         binding energy) fed into the SAME fitted formula the EmpiricalTreatment
                                         leg would use. Supported for all five legs: PhotoEmission
                                         (Empirical.UsingJAC), ImpactExcitation (bridged through
                                         Empirical.GivenEinsteinA), PhotoRecombination (Empirical.UsingJAC, via
                                         the Einstein-Milne relation to a mean-field photoionization cross
                                         section), and ImpactIonization/ThreeBodyRecombination
                                         (Empirical.Lotz1967, fed a mean-field AL-Field binding energy via the
                                         pSource keyword of Empirical.impactIonizationBindingEnergy rather than
                                         a tabulated one). Plasma.validateKineticChargeBalanceSupport checks the
                                         required approx for each leg and raises rather than guessing.
+ Plasma.StructureBasedTreatment   ... real input AND JAC's own cross-section/rate computation end-to-end; the
                                         only tier open to a GENERAL (non-classic) Plasma.ChargeStage. Carries the
                                         one knob that differs between models, an ImpactIonization.AbstractModel,
                                         read only by the ImpactIonization/ThreeBodyRecombination legs and ignored
                                         by the other three.

struct Plasma.AverageAtomOutcome ... the complete result of one average-atom computation, as named fields rather than as string keys into a Dict.

**THE DIVISION OF THE FIELDS IS THE SCHEME'S OWN DIVISION.**  The cell block is ALWAYS filled, because a
converged cell always has it;  every optional property is `Union{Nothing,...}` and is `nothing` exactly when
its switch was not set.  That distinction is the reason for the type:  a string-keyed Dict cannot tell
"this was not computed" from "this came out zero", and a conductivity of zero and an absent conductivity
mean entirely different things to whoever reads the number afterwards.

+ temperature          ::Float64   ... the temperature [a.u.] the cell was converged at.
+ massDensity          ::Float64   ... the mass density [g/cm^3].
+ radiusWS             ::Float64   ... the Wigner-Seitz cell radius [a.u.];  NOT the B-spline box, which is
                                       several times larger and must be -- see SelfConsistent's own note.
+ iCell                ::Int64     ... the grid index of the cell boundary, i.e. the last point with
                                       r <= radiusWS.  Every cell integral runs 1:iCell.
+ chemicalMu           ::Float64   ... the chemical potential [a.u.], fixed by neutrality of the cell.
+ meanCharge           ::Float64   ... Z*, the mean ionization, i.e. the free-electron count of the cell.
+ noBoundElectrons     ::Float64   ... the bound-electron count;  noBound + meanCharge = Z exactly.
+ radialDensity        ::Array{Float64,1}        ... the total electron density, carrying Z over the cell.
+ orbitals             ::Dict{Subshell, Orbital} ... the converged bound orbitals.
+ couplingParameter    ::Float64   ... the COUPLING PARAMETER Gamma = Z*^2/(R_WS kT):  the ratio of the
                                       typical Coulomb energy between neighbouring ions to their thermal
                                       energy.  Gamma << 1 is a weakly coupled (ideal) plasma, Gamma >> 1 a
                                       strongly coupled one, Gamma ~ 1 the warm-dense regime this model is
                                       for.
+ degeneracyParameter  ::Float64   ... the DEGENERACY PARAMETER Theta = kT/E_F with E_F the free-electron
                                       Fermi energy:  Theta << 1 is a degenerate electron gas, Theta >> 1 a
                                       classical one.  **Gamma and Theta say WHERE in the (rho,T) plane a
                                       number came from, so they are always reported and never optional** --
                                       a result quoted without them can be misused in a regime the model
                                       does not describe.
+ qValues              ::Union{Nothing, Array{Float64,1}}  ... momentum transfers of the form factor.
+ formFactors          ::Union{Nothing, Array{Float64,1}}  ... F(q), integrated over the cell.
+ piOmegas             ::Union{Nothing, Array{Float64,1}}  ... photon energies of the cross sections.
+ piCrossSections      ::Union{Nothing, Any}               ... bound-free cross sections per subshell.
+ scatteringFactor     ::Union{Nothing, Array{Float64,1}}  ... the imaginary scattering factor f_2.
+ massAttenuation      ::Union{Nothing, Array{Float64,1}}  ... mu/rho [cm^2/g].
+ condOmegas           ::Union{Nothing, Array{Float64,1}}  ... photon energies of the conductivity.
+ conductivity         ::Union{Nothing, Array{Float64,1}}  ... Re sigma(omega) [a.u.].
+ condLines            ::Union{Nothing, Any}               ... the individual box transitions and their
                                                               strengths, classified by kind.
+ condSumRules         ::Union{Nothing, Any}               ... the per-channel sum rules.
+ optical              ::Union{Nothing, Any}               ... eps(omega), n + ik, the absorption coefficient.
+ dcConductivity       ::Union{Nothing, Any}               ... sigma(0) and the collision rate, from the
                                                               scattering phase shifts.
+ meanOpacities        ::Union{Nothing, Any}               ... the Planck and Rosseland mean opacities.

JenaAtomicCalculator.Plasma.ChargeStage — Type

struct Plasma.ChargeStage ... one charge state ("ionization stage") entering a Plasma.KineticChargeBalanceScheme, carrying one or several reference configurations of a shared electron count and, for each of them, how many of its own lowest CI levels to retain. The CLASSIC form – exactly 2 configurations, 1 level each, built by the two-argument constructor below – is the only form a Plasma.EmpiricalTreatment or Plasma.SemiEmpiricalTreatment leg may touch, because Empirical's own rate formulas resolve a CONFIGURATION, not a level: asking one of them for a rate between two levels of the SAME configuration has no defined answer. A GENERAL stage (more configurations, or more than one level from a configuration) is open only to Plasma.StructureBasedTreatment, which computes from real levels throughout; Plasma.validateKineticChargeBalanceSupport raises, rather than guessing, if a general stage is paired with an Empirical/SemiEmpirical leg.

    A SECOND constraint on a general stage is NOT checked by Plasma itself: a cross-stage leg (ImpactIonization,
    PhotoRecombination, ThreeBodyRecombination) connects EVERY level of one stage to EVERY level of its
    neighbour, and each such pair must be a genuine single-electron capture/ionization -- the same shells
    occupied except for exactly one gained or lost. Re-deriving that check here would duplicate logic
    ThreeBodyRecombination.capturedSubshell already gets right; an invalid pair is left to raise there instead,
    which it does loudly and correctly (confirmed 22-Oct-2026: "found 2 subshells gained ... not exactly one").
    Choosing a general stage's configurations so that every pair against its neighbour IS a valid single-electron
    step is therefore the user's own responsibility, not a Plasma guarantee.

    See Plasma.AbstractRateTreatment.

+ configs    ::Array{Configuration,1}   ... reference configurations of this stage, lowest total energy expected
                                                first for the classic 2-configuration case (ground, then excited);
                                                for a general stage, order does not matter -- levels are sorted by
                                                computed energy once the Multiplet is built.
+ nLevels    ::Array{Int64,1}            ... for each entry of configs, how many of its own lowest CI levels to
                                                retain (same length as configs); the classic constructor sets [1,1].

struct Plasma.CollisionalRadiativeScheme <: Plasma.AbstractPlasmaScheme ... a small, closed-form (non-iterative) collisional-radiative population balance for one ion: for a generated set of its levels, computes how its population is distributed among them (a set of fractions summing to 1). Not a full multi-hundred-level CR code – for N levels, the steady-state balance (dn_i/dt = 0) is an N x N linear system, solved directly, once per (Te, Ne) point. Answers "how is this ion's population distributed among its levels", not "how much of this ion is present" – the latter (the ionization balance across charge states) is a separate question, answered separately (e.g. by Plasma.SahaBoltzmannScheme) if absolute densities are wanted. The ion itself (element and charge state) is not a field of this scheme: it is set entirely by the enclosing Plasma.Computation's nuclearModel (the element) and refConfigs (whose electron count against nuclearModel.Z fixes the charge state), exactly as for every other scheme.

+ NoExcitations     ::Int64
    ... how many electrons may be excited at once, relative to the ion's own ground configuration,
        when building its level set (1 = singles only, 2 = up to doubles, etc.) -- controls how rich
        a level set is generated.
+ upperShellNo      ::Int64
    ... the highest principal quantum number n allowed for an excited electron when building that
        level set -- controls how far up in energy the generated levels reach.
+ ieSettings        ::ImpactExcitation.Settings
    ... controls the electron-collisional (upward, excitation) rates between levels; the reverse
        (de-excitation) direction is obtained from these by detailed balance, not computed separately.
+ peSettings        ::PhotoEmission.Settings
    ... controls the radiative decay (A-value) rates between levels.
+ aiSettings        ::AutoIonization.Settings
    ... RESERVED, CURRENTLY UNUSED (verified 04-Aug-2026): this field is accepted and stored, but the
        driver never reads it and NO autoionization is computed -- every generated level is treated as
        purely bound (radiative + collisional only), as the @warn in Plasma.perform states. Setting it
        therefore changes nothing today. It is kept because the competing autoionization (electron-
        ejecting) decay is genuinely needed for any generated level that lies above the next charge
        state's ionization threshold -- a real possibility once NoExcitations/upperShellNo reach far
        enough up in energy, and the reason example-Je.jl branch b (boron-like Ne5+) cannot be trusted
        for its inner-shell block, which sits ~720 eV above threshold.
+ levelsFilenames   ::Array{String,1}
    ... candidate files (tried in order) that may contain a previously-generated, still-matching level
        set; [] (the default) means always regenerate. A fresh file is auto-written (name printed to
        screen) whenever no candidate matches, for adoption into a later run -- same manual-reuse
        workflow as Plasma.SahaBoltzmannScheme.isotopeFilenames.
+ ratesFilenames    ::Array{String,1}
    ... candidate files (tried in order) that may contain previously-computed, still-matching
        ImpactExcitation collision data (pre-thermal-average, so any scheme.ieSettings.temperatures
        list can reuse them); [] (the default) means always recompute. Same auto-write/manual-reuse
        workflow as levelsFilenames.
+ cacheDirectory    ::String
    ... subdirectory that freshly-generated levelsFilenames/ratesFilenames files are written into
        (created if missing); "" (the default) writes into the current directory. Only governs where
        FRESH files go -- candidates in levelsFilenames/ratesFilenames are looked up as given, so a
        cache file from elsewhere can always be reused directly.

JenaAtomicCalculator.Plasma.KineticChargeBalanceScheme — Type

struct Plasma.KineticChargeBalanceScheme <: Plasma.AbstractPlasmaScheme ... a kinetic (rate-based, non-LTE) population balance across SEVERAL adjacent charge states of one element – the cross-charge-state sibling to Plasma.CollisionalRadiativeScheme, which is confined by design to one fixed charge state. Every chargeStages[k] is connected to itself by PhotoEmission (radiative decay, excited -> ground) and ImpactExcitation (collisional excitation/de-excitation, both directions, by detailed balance from one computed/estimated rate), and to chargeStages[k+1] by ImpactIonization (up, ground -> ground) and by PhotoRecombination + ThreeBodyRecombination (down, ground -> ground, added into the same matrix entry). DielectronicRecombination is deliberately NOT included here (an explicit scope decision, not an oversight – its resonant capture-then-stabilize mechanism does not reduce to a single rate coefficient the way the other four processes do).

    EACH of the five legs carries its OWN Plasma.AbstractRateTreatment (peTreatment, ieTreatment, eiiTreatment,
    rrTreatment, tbrTreatment), so a run can mix all THREE tiers -- Empirical, SemiEmpirical, StructureBased --
    across the five processes freely, in any combination ("mixed use"), and EACH chargeStages[k] independently
    carries however many reference configurations/levels it needs (cf. Plasma.ChargeStage). A run in which every
    leg is Plasma.EmpiricalTreatment never calls a mean-field/CI computation at all and completes in well under a
    second; the moment ANY leg is Plasma.SemiEmpiricalTreatment or Plasma.StructureBasedTreatment, a reference
    Multiplet is generated for EVERY stage -- not only the stage(s) that leg touches -- since this is cheap here
    (one mean-field pass per stage) and a per-leg, per-stage decision of which levels to generate would add real
    bookkeeping for little saved time. An Empirical leg still uses its own Configuration-level formula regardless,
    so this costs extra computation, never wrong physics.

    NOT every combination of (treatment tier, chargeStage shape) is supported, and this is INTENTIONAL rather
    than an oversight: Plasma.EmpiricalTreatment and Plasma.SemiEmpiricalTreatment resolve rates at the
    CONFIGURATION level, so they are usable only on a CLASSIC Plasma.ChargeStage (exactly 2 configurations, 1
    level each, cf. Plasma.ChargeStage); a GENERAL (multi-configuration or multi-level) stage requires
    Plasma.StructureBasedTreatment on every leg that touches it. Plasma.SemiEmpiricalTreatment is supported on
    all five legs, each with its own required approx (see Plasma.SemiEmpiricalTreatment).
    Plasma.validateKineticChargeBalanceSupport checks every leg against every
    stage it touches BEFORE any Multiplet is generated, and RAISES -- naming the leg, the stage, and the nearest
    supported alternative -- rather than silently guessing at an unsupported combination or returning a wrong
    number (the error-over-guess pattern already used by corePolarization.doApply in module-PhotoEmission.jl).

+ chargeStages          ::Array{Plasma.ChargeStage,1}   ... the charge states, ordered from the LOWEST to the
                                                               HIGHEST charge (fewest to most electrons removed);
                                                               chargeStages[k] and chargeStages[k+1] must differ by

struct Plasma.SuperConfigurationMixtureOpacityScheme <: Plasma.AbstractPlasmaScheme ... a type for a MIXTURE of species, each possibly spread over SEVERAL of its own ionization stages, combined at a SHARED LTE temperature computation.settings.temperature into one PLANCK MEAN opacity over opacityOmegas. This is C9's own named remainder, item C20 in the competition list: "opacity for a realistic mixture, not one species" – EXTENDED 18-Oct-2026 to also cover the stage axis SuperConfigurationIonizationScheme covers for one species alone, so a mixture no longer has to be given a single hand-picked charge state per species.

    EACH SPECIES CARRIES ITS OWN `Nuclear.Model` AND OWN GRID PER STAGE, built automatically
    (`Basics.recommendedGrid`) rather than shared from `computation` -- a mixture is, by definition, more
    than one element (and, now, each element possibly more than one charge state), so
    `computation.nuclearModel`/`computation.grid` (sized for ONE species at ONE stage) cannot be reused
    without breaking Rule 12's box-matching requirement for whichever (species,stage) is not the one
    `computation` was built for. `computation.nuclearModel`/`computation.grid` are therefore UNUSED by
    this scheme, the same way `computation.refConfigs` is already unused by
    `SuperConfigurationOpacityScheme`'s own `refConfigs` field.

    THE IONIZATION BALANCE IS SOLVED FIRST, COUPLED ACROSS EVERY SPECIES AT ONCE -- the genuinely new
    physics here, not a wiring exercise: `SuperConfigurationIonizationScheme`'s own charge-neutrality
    equation assumes ONE species supplies every electron, which is wrong the moment a species is a
    trace component of the mixture -- its own ionization balance then depends on an electron density
    set mostly by everyone ELSE. The SAME grand-canonical fixed-point iteration is reused, with the
    charge-neutrality sum lifted to range over every (species, stage) pair:

        n_e  =  n_ion,total * SUM_species  numberFractions[s] * SUM_stages_i  charge[s,i] * p[s,i]

    where `p[s,i]` is species `s`'s OWN Boltzmann population of stage `i`, normalized to 1 WITHIN that
    species (`SUM_i p[s,i] = 1`) at the SHARED chemical potential `mu_e` every species sees -- each
    species keeps its own, purely numerical energy reference for the exponentials (Eref_s), which never
    needs to agree ACROSS species since it cancels out of that species' own normalization. A SINGLE-
    STAGE species has `p[s,1] = 1` identically, whatever `mu_e` is -- which is exactly why the
    backward-compatible constructor below reproduces the ORIGINAL (17-Oct-2026) scheme's numbers
    unchanged: the balance above is trivial when there is only one state to belong to.

    THE MIXING RULE builds on that balance in two stages: WITHIN a species, its own stages' Stage-3
    `kappa_{s,i}(omega)` (each built with EXACTLY `SuperConfigurationOpacityScheme`'s own machinery) are
    weighted by that stage's OWN population `p[s,i]`; ACROSS species, by `numberFractions`:

        kappa_s(omega)    =  SUM_i  p[s,i] * kappa_{s,i}(omega)
        kappa_mix(omega)  =  SUM_s  numberFractions[s] * kappa_s(omega)

    THE PLANCK MEAN is the standard dimensionless-weight form, written in terms of `x = omega/kT`:

        kappa_P  =  INTEGRAL  kappa_mix(x) * w_P(x) dx,     w_P(x) = (15/pi^4) * x^3/(e^x-1)

    normalized so `INTEGRAL w_P(x) dx = 1` over `x in (0,infinity)` -- the standard check this scheme's
    own test uses: a FREQUENCY-INDEPENDENT `kappa_mix` must return exactly that same constant as its
    Planck mean, since a weighted average of a constant is that constant. The integral is evaluated by
    the trapezoidal rule over `opacityOmegas` itself, so the grid must be wide and fine enough to
    resolve `w_P`'s own peak near `x ~ 3-4` AND every species' own line widths -- not merely wide
    enough for the lines alone, as a single-species `opacityOmegas` choice would be.

    an oversight: a Rosseland mean is a HARMONIC average, dominated by whichever frequencies are LEAST
    opaque, and a `kappa(omega)` built from E1 lines ALONE -- no bound-free or free-free continuum --
    leaves every frequency between lines a true gap with nothing sensible to divide into. Measured, not

JenaAtomicCalculator.Plasma.computeConductivityLines — Method

Plasma.computeConductivityLines(scfResult::NamedTuple, temp::Float64, radiusWS::Float64, grid::Radial.Grid, primitives::Bsplines.Primitives) ... computes every dipole-allowed transition between the box states of a converged average atom and the conductivity it carries; an Array{Plasma.ConductivityLine,1} is returned, sorted by omega.

    This is the average-atom Kubo-Greenwood formula of Johnson, Guet & Bertsch, JQSRT 99, 327 (2006), Eq.
    (13), evaluated in the B-spline box.  Their Eqs. (14), (16) and (17) are the free-free, bound-bound and
    bound-free parts of the same sum;  here they are not separated, because in a box they are the same object
    -- a list of transitions between box states -- and only the SIGNS of the two energies say which name a
    transition goes by.  **THAT IS THE SIMPLIFICATION THE BOX BUYS.**  Johnson's free-free and bound-free
    terms carry an integral over the continuum energy with energy-normalised continuum functions;  in a box
    the energy normalisation cancels the level spacing and the integral becomes a plain sum over box states,
    exactly as it does for the density.

    Each transition contributes `sigma_ab = (pi/3 Omega) (f_a - f_b) omega_ab |<kappa_a||C^(1)||kappa_b>|^2
    R_ab^2`, with `R_ab = int (P_a P_b + Q_a Q_b) r dr` the length-form radial integral and `f` the Fermi
    factor.  The value stored is the INTEGRATED strength of the line, i.e. its contribution to
    `int sigma d omega`;  `Plasma.computeConductivitySpectrum` folds the lines into a spectrum.

    **THE BOX WALL SCATTERS, SO THE FREE-FREE PART NEEDS THE SAME SUBTRACTION THE DENSITY NEEDS.**  A free
    particle has no free-free absorption at all:  between two exact free waves normalised on the energy scale
    the dipole matrix element is a delta function in the energy, so it vanishes for any omega > 0, and
    Johnson's free-free l-sum converges because a high-l continuum pair barely feels the potential.  A BOX
    state is not a free wave -- the wall supplies the momentum -- so free-free transitions between box states
    are non-zero however high l goes, and the sum rule then grows without limit:  measured 11-Oct-2026 on
    aluminium at 0.27 g/cm^3, N_ff came out 3.13 at lMax = 3 and 6.82 at lMax = 5.  Called with
    `useFreeSpectra = true` this routine returns the same list for the FREE (V = 0) box, and the difference of
    the two is the physical free-free conductivity;  `Plasma.computeConductivitySumRules` and
    `Plasma.computeConductivitySpectrum` both take the two lists and subtract.

    **THE RELATIVISTIC ANGULAR FACTOR REPLACES JOHNSON'S `l_>`, AND REDUCES TO IT.**  Johnson's model is
    non-relativistic -- his Eq. (1) is the Schroedinger equation -- so his line strength is `l_> R^2`.  Summed
    over the two spin-orbit partners of each l, JAC's `AngularMomentum.CL_reduced_me` gives exactly `2 l_>`
    (checked for l = 0...4), and the factor of two is the spin that his prefactor carries separately.  So the
    form above is his wherever fine structure does not matter, and better where it does.

    Validated 11-Oct-2026 against their Table 2, aluminium at 0.27 g/cm^3 and T = 5 eV:  the four
    bound-bound lines come out at -0.24 %, -0.14 %, -0.42 % and +3.4 % (3s -> 3p, the smallest omega and the
    one most sensitive to the 3s and 3p energies), and the sum rule gives N_bb = 0.504 against their 0.493.

JenaAtomicCalculator.Plasma.computeConductivitySpectrum — Method

Plasma.computeConductivitySpectrum(lines::Array{Plasma.ConductivityLine,1}, omegas::Array{Float64,1}, broadening::Float64) ... folds the conductivity lines into a spectrum on the given photon energies; a sigmas::Array{Float64,1} of the real part of the conductivity [a.u.] is returned.

    Every transition of the box is a delta line, and a spectrum needs a width.  Each line is folded with a
    normalised Lorentzian of half-width `broadening`, `L(w) = broadening/(pi ((w-w_ab)^2 + broadening^2))`,
    which preserves `int sigma dw` exactly and so leaves the sum rules of
    `Plasma.computeConductivitySumRules` untouched.  Johnson, Guet & Bertsch (2006) do the same for their
    bound-bound lines, with the width taken to be the electron-ion collision rate.

    **THE WIDTH IS A MODEL AND NOT A RESULT**, which is the one thing to keep in view when reading a
    spectrum from this routine.  A broadening much smaller than the box level spacing shows the comb of box
    states rather than a continuum;  one much larger washes out real structure, such as the opening of an
    ionization edge.  The box spacing of the continuum near an energy eps is about `pi^2 n/R^2` with
    `n = R sqrt(2 eps)/pi`, so a sensible choice is a few times that.

    `freeLines`, if given, are folded in the same way and SUBTRACTED, which removes the box-wall artifact
    from the free-free part;  see `Plasma.computeConductivityLines`.

JenaAtomicCalculator.Plasma.computeConductivitySumRules — Method

Plasma.computeConductivitySumRules(lines::Array{Plasma.ConductivityLine,1}, nFree::Float64, radiusWS::Float64) ... evaluates the conductivity sum rule of Johnson, Guet & Bertsch (2006), Eq. (15), separately for the three kinds of transition; a NamedTuple (nBoundBound, nBoundFree, nFreeFree, nTotal, nFree, gamma) is returned.

    `int sigma(omega) d omega = pi N_free / (2 Omega)` in atomic units, so each transition carries
    `(2 Omega/pi) sigma_ab` electrons and the three partial sums are the effective electron numbers that
    Johnson quotes -- `N_bb = 0.493` for aluminium at 0.27 g/cm^3 and 5 eV.

    **THE SUM RULE IS WHAT FIXES THE ONE FREE PARAMETER OF THE MODEL.**  The free-free conductivity diverges
    as `1/omega^2` towards zero frequency, because the radial matrix element between two nearly degenerate
    continuum states grows without bound;  in reality a finite lifetime cuts it off.  Johnson mocks that up
    with a Drude factor `omega^2/(gamma^2 + omega^2)` and chooses `gamma` so that the FREE-FREE part of the
    sum rule returns the number of free electrons.  `gamma` is then the mean electron-ion collision rate, and
    the returned value is that choice:  `gamma` such that `sum_ff sigma_ab omega_ab^2/(gamma^2+omega_ab^2)`
    equals `pi n_free/(2 Omega)`.  For their aluminium case `gamma = 0.0466 a.u. = 1.27 eV`.

JenaAtomicCalculator.Plasma.computeMeanOpacities — Method

Plasma.computeMeanOpacities(omegas::Array{Float64,1}, sigmas::Array{Float64,1}, temp::Float64, massDensity::Float64) ... forms the Planck and Rosseland mean opacities of the plasma from its conductivity spectrum; a NamedTuple (planck, rosseland, kappa, weightPlanck, weightRosseland, xValues) is returned, the two means in cm^2/g and kappa the spectral mass absorption coefficient they are formed from.

    **THE TWO MEANS ARE DIFFERENT FUNCTIONALS AND NOT TWO WEIGHTINGS OF ONE, which is the whole physics.**
    With `x = omega/kT`,

        kappa_P   = (15/pi^4)   int kappa(x)     x^3 / (e^x - 1)       dx          ... ARITHMETIC
        1/kappa_R = (15/4 pi^4) int [1/kappa(x)] x^4 e^x / (e^x - 1)^2 dx          ... HARMONIC

    The Planck mean is dominated by the frequencies where the plasma absorbs MOST and is the one to use
    when the radiation escapes;  the Rosseland mean is dominated by the WINDOWS where it absorbs least,
    because radiation leaks through them, and is the one to use when the plasma is optically thick.  So
    `rosseland <= planck` always, a harmonic mean never exceeding its arithmetic counterpart, and the two
    can differ by orders of magnitude rather than by a factor.

    **AN ARITHMETIC SUM CARRYING THE ROSSELAND WEIGHT IS NOT A ROSSELAND MEAN**, and that error has been
    made in this project before -- `simulateRosselandOpacities` formed `sum_i w_i kappa_i` while calling the
    result Rosseland.  The normalisations above are therefore checked rather than trusted:  a CONSTANT
    `kappa` must return that same constant from BOTH means, since both weights integrate to one, and that
    identity is asserted by the suite.

    **WHY THIS BELONGS TO THE AVERAGE ATOM AND NOT TO A LINE LIST.**  A correct Rosseland mean of bound-bound
    lines ALONE is identically ZERO: an empty frequency bin means `kappa = 0` there, and one perfectly
    transparent window short-circuits a harmonic mean.  An average atom has no empty bins -- the free-free
    and bound-free channels absorb at every frequency -- so here the harmonic mean is finite and meaningful,
    which it cannot be made to be for a line list without an added continuum.

    **NO FURTHER STIMULATED-EMISSION FACTOR IS APPLIED, and this is the one convention to check before
    comparing with another code.**  An opacity built from a bare cross section needs the factor
    `(1 - e^{-x})`;  the Kubo-Greenwood conductivity does NOT, because its own `(f_a - f_b)` already IS
    absorption minus stimulated emission.  Applying both is a classic double correction and would depress
    the means by up to that factor at small x.

    The absorption coefficient comes from `Plasma.computeOpticalProperties` (its `absorption` field,
    `2 omega k/c` in inverse Bohr radii) and is divided by the mass density to give cm^2/g.  Scattering is
    NOT included:  a Rosseland mean for a hot, thin plasma should carry at least the Thomson term, and this
    routine deliberately leaves it out rather than guessing a value -- so the number is an ABSORPTION mean.

JenaAtomicCalculator.Plasma.computeOpticalProperties — Method

Plasma.computeOpticalProperties(omegas::Array{Float64,1}, sigmas::Array{Float64,1}) ... turns the real part of the conductivity into the optical constants of the plasma; a NamedTuple (sigmaImag, epsReal, epsImag, refIndex, extinction, absorption) of Array{Float64,1} is returned, each on the given photon energies.

    The imaginary part of the conductivity follows from the real part by the Kramers-Kronig relation,
    `Im sigma(w) = -(2w/pi) P int_0^inf Re sigma(w') dw'/(w'^2 - w^2)`, and then, in atomic units and
    following Johnson, Guet & Bertsch (2006), Section 4.1,

        Re eps = 1 - 4 pi Im sigma / w,     Im eps = 4 pi Re sigma / w,     n + i k = sqrt(eps),

    with the refractive index and the extinction coefficient
    `n = sqrt((|eps| + Re eps)/2)`, `k = sqrt((|eps| - Re eps)/2)`, and the absorption coefficient
    `alpha = 2 w k / c` [1/a_o].

    **THE PRINCIPAL VALUE IS TAKEN BY SUBTRACTION AND IS THEREFORE EXACT RATHER THAN REGULARISED.**
    `P int_0^inf dw'/(w'^2-w^2)` vanishes identically, so subtracting `Re sigma(w)` from the numerator
    leaves an integrand with no pole and changes nothing;  at `w' = w` the integrand is the derivative
    `sigma'(w)/(2w)`, which is taken from the neighbouring points.

    **AND THE ANSWER IS ONLY AS GOOD AS THE RANGE OF `omegas`.**  The dispersion integral runs to infinity,
    so a grid that stops below the K edge of the element silently omits the bound-free strength above it and
    the refractive index comes out wrong at every frequency, not only near the edge.  Carry `omegas` well
    past the deepest ionization threshold, and log-spaced, since the integrand spans decades.

JenaAtomicCalculator.Plasma.drudeConductivity — Method

Plasma.drudeConductivity(nFree::Float64, radiusWS::Float64, gamma::Float64, omegas::Array{Float64,1}) ... returns the free-electron (Drude) conductivity sigma(w) = sigma(0) gamma^2/(gamma^2 + w^2) of the cell on the given photon energies; a sigmas::Array{Float64,1} [a.u.] is returned.

    **THIS IS A MODEL WITH ONE PARAMETER, AND IT IS HERE BECAUSE THE BOX CANNOT DO THE JOB.**  The free-free
    conductivity of a plasma carries the whole free-electron oscillator strength, and it carries it at photon
    energies of order the electron-ion collision rate -- below the level spacing of any affordable box, and
    in a region where the free-free subtraction of `Plasma.computeConductivitySpectrum` changes sign.  So the
    free-electron response is supplied here in closed form instead of computed.

    Its AMPLITUDE is not free:  the conductivity sum rule `int sigma dw = pi N_free/(2 Omega)` gives
    `sigma(0) = N_free/(Omega gamma)` with no adjustable factor left.  Only `gamma` is a choice -- and since
    11-Oct-2026 it need not be a choice at all:  `Plasma.dcConductivity` computes it from the average atom's
    own scattering phase shifts, so the whole optical chain can be run with nothing supplied from outside.  Johnson, Guet & Bertsch (2006) obtain
    `gamma = 0.0466 a.u. = 1.27 eV` for aluminium at 0.27 g/cm^3 and 5 eV by imposing that same sum rule on
    their (divergent) free-free integral, and quote `sigma(0) = 0.0262 a.u. = 0.121 (mOhm m)^-1` against
    0.125 from the Ziman formula.  The closed form above gives 0.0286 at their gamma -- 9 % high, the
    difference being that their value comes from the damped Kubo-Greenwood integrand and not from a pure
    Drude shape.

    phase convention cannot matter.  Contrast the free-free dipole amplitude, where the box boundary enters
    the integrand itself;  that is the difference between this route to the DC conductivity and the one that
